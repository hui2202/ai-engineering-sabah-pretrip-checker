# Week 1 — Project Scope: Sabah Pre-Trip Checker

| Field | Details |
|---|---|
| **Project Name** | Sabah Pre-Trip Checker |
| **Domain** | Travel & Tourism — pre-trip requirements for travel in Sabah, Malaysia |
| **Problem Statement** | Travellers to Sabah must satisfy procedural requirements such as climbing and diving permits, daily quotas, booking windows, pre-arrival registration, nationality-based fees and seasonal closures. These requirements are scattered across official documents and mixed with outdated or conflicting information online, so travellers often discover them too late. AI engineering makes it possible to build a document-grounded assistant that understands a traveller's free-text trip description, retrieves the applicable requirements from official sources with citations, and calculates the deadline for each requirement from the travel dates. |
| **Evidence of the Problem** | **Evidence 1:** In an exploratory test (October 2026), ChatGPT and Google AI were both asked how to prepare for a Mount Kinabalu climb in March 2027. Neither mentioned the online self check-in, which becomes available five days before the climb and must be completed so that each climber can prepare the indemnity form required at registration. Google AI also urged users to secure 2027 slots immediately and pay a deposit, claiming that agents had already started taking 2027 bookings, although the official Sabah Parks page stated that, as of 19 September 2026, bookings for the 2027 climbing season had not opened.<br>**Source / citation:** Own test log (`docs/evidence/ai_tool_tests.md`); Sabah Parks Self Check-In page, https://reservation.sabahparks.org.my/v2/mountain-climbing/checkin; Sabah Parks Mount Kinabalu Climbing guide (2027 Booking Status, Booking Procedure and Documents to bring), https://sabahparks.org.my/services/mount-kinabalu-climbing<br><br>**Evidence 2:** Online information about regulated sites is inconsistent. Websites report different daily climbing permit limits for Mount Kinabalu (135, 163 and 185) and different daily limits for Sipadan Island (120, 176 and 250), while the official Sabah Parks climbing guide does not state any of the Mount Kinabalu figures.<br>**Source / citation:** Mount Kinabalu: https://sabahguide.pages.dev/activities/permits/ranau/; https://www.mountkinabalu.com/news/mount-kinabalu-reopening-latest-sop-guidelines-effective-from-1st-october-2021; https://myhometown.com.my/?p=2381. Sipadan: https://figaronautisme.meteoconsult.fr/actus-nautisme-loisirs/2026-03-05/86155-tourisme-sous-controle-comment-obtenir-un-permis-pour-plonger-a-sipadan-joyau-protege-de-borneo; https://www.southeastasiadiving.com/blog/sipadan-diving-guide-permits-best-season-and-what-to-expect; https://www.amazingborneo.com/index.php/package/pom-pom-island-sipadan-pom-pom-resort. Official guide: https://sabahparks.org.my/services/mount-kinabalu-climbing |
| **Initial Related Work (at least 3)** | **Study / solution 1:** TravelPlanner — a benchmark for real-world travel planning with language agents<br>**Source / citation:** Xie, J. et al. (2024). *TravelPlanner: A Benchmark for Real-World Planning with Language Agents*. ICML 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01622<br>**Key limitation:** Shows that LLM agents struggle to satisfy multiple constraints (GPT-4 reached a 0.6% final pass rate), but focuses on general itinerary planning rather than official procedural requirements and deadlines.<br><br>**Study / solution 2:** General-purpose AI assistants (ChatGPT, Google AI Overview)<br>**Source / citation:** Own exploratory tests, October 2026 (`docs/evidence/ai_tool_tests.md`)<br>**Key limitation:** Answer well-known risks (e.g. monsoon) well, but miss procedural requirements stated in official documents; source quality varies between tools (Google AI cited an Instagram post and tour operators rather than official sources); and neither calculates deadlines.<br><br>**Study / solution 3:** Official Sabah Parks website and its AI assistant TAVI, alongside travel information websites<br>**Source / citation:** Sabah Parks (https://www.sabahparks.org.my); own test of TAVI, October 2026 (`docs/evidence/ai_tool_tests.md`); tour operator and travel blog websites<br>**Key limitation:** Official information is spread across separate pages and documents, and unofficial websites are often outdated and contradict each other. TAVI answers specific step-by-step questions well with cited excerpts (e.g. how to book, what to do after booking), but when asked a trip-level question (how to prepare for a Mount Kinabalu climb in March 2027) it replied that it did not have enough information, although the answer is on Sabah Parks' own guide. Users must therefore already know which questions to ask; TAVI does not convert requirements into dated deadlines, and it covers only Sabah Parks content, not other agencies' requirements. |
| **Engineering Gap / Limitation** | **Gap / Limitation:** General-purpose AI assistants can warn about widely known travel risks, but they do not systematically check procedural requirements against official documents, while the official site-specific assistant answers only when users ask the right step-by-step questions and covers a single agency. Source quality also varies between general-purpose tools, with some answers relying on social media posts and tour operators rather than official sources, so the same question can receive contradictory answers from different tools. Neither type of assistant calculates when each requirement must be completed based on the traveller's dates. |
| **Initial Project Objectives (2–3)** | **O1:** To design a document-grounded pre-trip requirement assistant for travel in Sabah based on official sources.<br>**O2:** To develop the assistant with retrieval, source citation, rule-based deadline calculation and scope guardrails.<br>**O3:** To evaluate the assistant against a general-purpose LLM baseline in terms of requirement detection rate, citation correctness, deadline accuracy and out-of-scope refusal. |
| **Target Users** | Domestic and international travellers planning independent trips to Sabah, particularly those visiting regulated sites such as Mount Kinabalu, Sipadan Island and the marine parks. |
| **Data Sources (at least 2)** | **Source 1:** Sabah Parks booking Terms and Conditions (PDF) — URL: https://reservation.sabahparks.org.my/files/TermsandCondition.pdf<br>**Source 2:** Sabah Parks Mount Kinabalu Climbing guide (booking procedure, fees, itinerary, changes and cancellations) — URL: https://sabahparks.org.my/services/mount-kinabalu-climbing<br>**Source 3:** OpenDOSM Monthly Air Pollution dataset (CC BY 4.0, supporting data for seasonal haze patterns) — URL: https://open.dosm.gov.my/data-catalogue/air_pollution<br>**Source 4:** GEC Haze Safety Advisory for Outdoor Activities (PDF) — URL: https://gec.org.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/20250723_haze_a4_poster_01.pdf |
| **Similar to EduAdvisor** | Answers questions grounded in official documents, provides a source reference for each answer, and declines queries outside its defined scope. |
| **Different from EduAdvisor** | Proactively lists all requirements for a whole trip from one description, instead of answering one question at a time; extracts structured trip details (destinations, dates, activities, traveller nationality) from free text; calculates deadlines with deterministic rules rather than the language model; applies rules according to traveller category (e.g. Malaysian vs international); and must handle time-sensitive information by showing source dates and flagging outdated rules. |
| **Responsible AI Risks (at least 2)** | **Risk 1 — Hallucination / outdated information:** The system states an invented or outdated permit rule → the traveller misses a booking window or is refused entry.<br>**Risk 2 — Fairness:** Requirement detection is less accurate for international travellers than for Malaysian travellers → unequal quality of guidance between user groups.<br>**Risk 3 — Safety / scope:** Users ask for medical advice (e.g. altitude sickness medication) or visa eligibility decisions → the system must not give medical or legal advice and must redirect users to official sources. |
